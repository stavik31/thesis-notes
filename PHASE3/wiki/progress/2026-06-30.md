# 2026-06-30 — Stage 5 bug fixed; bbox limitation surfaced

> **⚠ CORRECTED 2026-07-02.** The "Stage 5 bug fixed" claim below was *necessary but
> insufficient*. The `risk_scores.csv` swap was a real fix (it stopped edge weights reading
> a binarised hotspot file), but a **second, independent** cause remained: routes were still
> **100% identical across vehicle types**. The "routes now differentiate on risk" table below
> shows the *reported risk number* differing across ranks **of one type** — not routes
> differing **between types**. Root cause (uncapped per-type normalisation over an
> exposure-artifact max) and the fix are in [[wiki/progress/2026-07-02]] and
> [[wiki/concepts/routing-risk-normalization]].

## What got done

**Stage 5 bug fixed.** Root cause: `load_risk()` was loading `risk_surface_filtered.csv`
as edge weights. Stage 4's filtered surface zeroes out 85% of segments (below the 85th-percentile
threshold), so edge weights collapsed to pure travel time for most of the network. Yen's k-shortest
then found k near-identical travel-time paths with negligible risk variation (delta ~0.01–0.02).

Fix applied in `code/stage5_routing/route.py` → `load_risk()`:
- Edge weights now load from `risk_scores.csv` (raw GAT output — continuous risk surface)
- Hotspot flags still load from `risk_surface_filtered.csv` (85th-pct threshold — map overlay only)
- Both are merged on `segment_id` + `vehicle_type`; `is_hotspot` fills NA as False

**Stage 5 re-run confirmed working.** Routes now differentiate on risk. Test run
(51.5074,-0.1278 → 51.5200,-0.0900, car, k=3):

| Rank | Dist | Time | Total risk | Hotspots | MCDM score |
|------|------|------|------------|----------|------------|
| 1 | 3.965 km | 2.583 min | 0.131 | 64 | 0.000 |
| 2 | 3.966 km | 2.583 min | 0.173 | 65 | 0.600 |
| 3 | 3.971 km | 2.587 min | 0.139 | 65 | 0.673 |

Rank 1 vs rank 2 = 32% risk difference. MCDM correctly ranks rank 1 as the winner.

**Bbox limitation surfaced and discussed.** The routing clips to a bounding box around the
O-D pair (default 50% padding) for performance — loading the full 3.96M-node national graph
into Yen's would be impractical. This means routes that go outside the rectangle are never
considered, which is a real usability limitation for a production system.

**Decision: bbox stays, documented as prototype limitation.** Two reasons:
1. The thesis evaluation compares vehicle-type routes against each other for the same O-D pair.
   Both types use the same bbox → vehicle-type divergence is still real and measurable. The
   core thesis claim is not invalidated.
2. Production fix (contraction hierarchies, OSRM/Valhalla) is out of scope. Sarraf and others
   also scope routing to subgraphs in research prototypes — this is standard.

Document in the thesis: the contribution is the risk layer and the divergence result, not the
routing engine. Bbox is a known computational shortcut.

**Practically:** for visually distinct routes in demos, use longer O-D pairs with natural
geographic separation (e.g., routes that cross the Thames, or inter-city routes where different
road corridors exist).
