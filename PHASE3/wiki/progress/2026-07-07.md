# 2026-07-07 — Grouping-robustness + CLQ certification (direction-justification battery)

**Context.** Mid-way through the Stage 3 rewrite (see [[wiki/build/stage-3-cluster-share-engine]]),
a peer raised two challenges that needed empirical answers before continuing:

1. **"This is a combinatorial / NP-complete clustering problem — you're not clustering,
   you're *grouping*; different groupings mean different things, so what defines it?"**
   → the **Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP)**. The optimal contiguity-constrained
   version (**max-p-regions**) *is* NP-hard, which is exactly why we use a linear-time
   deterministic heuristic (densest-first BFS). NP-hardness does **not** apply because we
   never search the partition space — we construct one partition in O(V+E). The real,
   legitimate residue is MAUP: does the result depend on the arbitrary grouping? That is a
   *sensitivity* question, answered by experiment, not argument.

2. **"Even if robust, you need a proven / industry-standard metric to certify the results."**
   → identified the field-standard metrics (below) and ran the **Colocation Quotient (CLQ)**,
   the model-free premise certifier used by the niche's founding papers (Lee 2018, Hu 2018).

All tests are ad-hoc validation in `code/tests/cluster_risk/` (not the pipeline). They
**justify the direction**; they will be re-run cleanly inside Stage 6 later.

---

## Test 1 — Grouping THRESHOLD sensitivity (`sens_threshold`, via `run_xgb.py --min-crashes`)

Genuine **Manchester spatial holdout** (city excluded from clustering + training; eval on
its 2,680 crash-bearing segments). Swept `MIN_CRASHES ∈ {20, 30, 50}`.

**Cross-type ρ** (lower = more per-type divergence):

| MIN_CRASHES | statistical | XGBoost | car clusters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | −0.079 | **−0.032** | 75,067 |
| 30 | 0.160 | **−0.038** | 63,814 |
| 50 | 0.026 | **−0.071** | 52,866 |

**Validity vs real target crashes** (Spearman, per type car/moto/cycle/lgv/hgv; mean):

| surface | car | moto | cycle | lgv | hgv | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stat @20 | −.006 | −.030 | .103 | −.001 | .115 | .036 |
| ML @20 | .006 | .050 | .064 | .047 | .108 | **.055** |
| stat @30 | .048 | −.002 | .009 | .018 | .126 | .040 |
| ML @30 | .013 | .044 | .167 | .093 | .131 | **.090** |
| stat @50 | .031 | .019 | .123 | −.016 | .115 | .054 |
| ML @50 | .049 | .039 | .152 | .087 | .135 | **.092** |

**Findings.**
- **Divergence robust to threshold:** XGBoost ρ stays −0.03…−0.07 while car cluster count
  swings 75k→53k. The headline (divergence) does not depend on the threshold.
- **Model's divergence is more stable than the statistic's:** stat ρ wobbles (−0.079 →
  **0.160** → 0.026); ML ρ does not — a small extra argument for training.
- **ML beats the statistic on mean validity at every threshold** (.055/.090/.092 vs
  .036/.040/.054); per-type wins 3/5, 4/5, **5/5** at 20/30/50. An earlier "wash" flag at
  mc=20 was threshold-specific (finest clusters) and did **not** generalise.
- **Caveat:** one noisy single-city holdout (2,680 segs) — per-type numbers flip around
  (e.g. stat cycle .103→.009→.123). National spatial-CV + precision@top-X% will be steadier.

## Test 2 — Grouping SCHEME sensitivity (`sens_scheme.py`)

**National**, 158,237 crash-bearing eval segments, `MIN_CRASHES=30`, everything downstream
of clustering **identical to `run_xgb.py`** (same features, share target, XGBoost, eval).
Only the `cluster_id` assignment changes:

| scheme | what it changes | stat ρ | XGBoost ρ | clusters/type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bfs_dense` | the real engine (densest-first) | −0.075 | **−0.076** | 64,683 … 18,260 |
| `bfs_random` | seeds in RANDOM order | −0.080 | **−0.080** | 67,245 … 18,748 |
| `grid` | **no network**; lon/lat bins, type-identical | −0.040 | **−0.050** | 33,257 (all types) |

**Validity (XGBoost)** car/moto/cycle/lgv/hgv:

| scheme | car | moto | cycle | lgv | hgv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bfs_dense | .130 | .236 | .265 | .117 | .136 |
| bfs_random | .124 | .237 | .262 | .116 | .118 |
| grid | .112 | .257 | .291 | .136 | .165 |

(stat validity: bfs_dense .107/.186/.223/.064/.109 · bfs_random .092/.185/.221/.052/.089 ·
grid .094/.155/.225/.024/.049 — ML > stat on all types, all schemes.)

**Findings.**
- **Construction ORDER is irrelevant:** `bfs_random` ≈ `bfs_dense` to two decimals. The
  greedy heuristic's path through the graph does not shape the answer.
- **The grid is the smoking gun:** grid cells are **identical across all 5 types** (no
  per-type clustering at all), yet surfaces still diverge (ρ=−0.05). ⇒ **divergence
  originates in the SHARE TARGET, not in the clustering.** Clean causal attribution.
- **Grid is competitive/better on national validity** (cycle .291 vs .265, hgv .165 vs .136).
  Implication: the elaborate network clustering is **not** required for divergence/validity —
  a standard spatial grid would do. BUT network-BFS keeps one advantage grid lacks:
  **propagation into zero-history held-out areas** (BFS absorbs a never-seen city into
  clusters reaching in from outside; a grid cell that is all-holdout has no signal). So keep
  BFS if Stage-6 generalisation-to-new-regions matters; a grid is a simpler defensible
  alternative otherwise. **To confirm: rerun grid-vs-BFS inside the Stage-6 spatial CV.**
- **Caveat:** national **in-sample** (not held out), single run per scheme.

**Combined verdict (Tests 1+2): the MAUP concern is answered.** Across 3 thresholds AND 3
fundamentally different schemes, cross-type ρ never leaves ≈[−0.03, −0.08]. The divergence
is a property of the **data + share target**, not the partition.

## Test 3 — CLQ premise certification (`clq.py`)

**Colocation Quotient** (Leslie & Kronenfeld 2011; Lee 2018, Hu 2018) on **raw** map-matched
crash points — model-free (no clustering, no share, no XGBoost). 885,297 points
(car 640,231 · moto 84,960 · cycle 80,921 · lgv 57,833 · hgv 21,352), projected to BNG
metres, k=10 nearest neighbours, 199 label-permutations. CLQ<1 = spatial segregation
(divergence); >1 = attraction; permutation `p_low<0.01` = significant segregation.

**CLQ matrix** (row A → col B):

| A→B | car | moto | cycle | lgv | hgv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| car | 1.04 | 0.89 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.95 |
| moto | 0.90 | 1.79 | **1.03** | 0.95 | 0.76 |
| cycle | 0.88 | **1.02** | 2.15 | 0.89 | **0.59** |
| lgv | 0.94 | 0.94 | 0.90 | 1.81 | **1.20** |
| hgv | 0.95 | 0.74 | **0.57** | **1.19** | 4.67 |

- Self-CLQ (diagonal), all significant: cycle 2.15, lgv 1.81, moto 1.79, **hgv 4.67** — every
  type self-clusters (HGV extremely). Car ≈1.0 because cars are 72% of points (that IS the
  base rate — correct behaviour).
- **Mean cross-type CLQ = 0.905.** 14/20 ordered pairs significantly segregated (p<0.01).

**Key finding — divergence is a 3-GROUP structure, not uniform 5-way:**
- **motorcycle ↔ cycle COLOCATE** (CLQ 1.02–1.03, ns) — urban two-wheeled VRUs.
- **lgv ↔ hgv COLOCATE** (CLQ 1.19–1.20) — freight on shared corridors.
- Everything **across** the car / two-wheeler / freight groups is significantly segregated,
  strongest **cycle ↔ hgv = 0.57–0.59** (cyclists and lorries crash in near-disjoint places).

**Implications.**
- **Premise certified, with correct scope:** vehicle types crash in significantly different
  places (model-free, permutation-tested, field-standard statistic). ✅
- **Answers the Zhu-2025 "group ≠ type" critique** (flagged in `raw/papers/carry_over/T-ITS-deepread.md`):
  divergence is cleanest at the **group** level; two within-group type pairs colocate. Report
  this honestly — it is stronger than claiming uniform 5-way orthogonality.
- **⚠ Refines/softens the prior "near-orthogonal, ρ≈0 for ALL types, 4% overlap" claim** in
  [[wiki/concepts/vehicle-type-risk-divergence]]: moto/cycle and lgv/hgv are NOT orthogonal.
  Concept page flagged; full integration pending.
- **Routing unaffected:** value lives in the strongly divergent pairs (cycle↔hgv etc.);
  colocated pairs simply route similarly (honest, correct).

## Industry-standard evaluation metrics identified (for the writeup / Stage 6)

Mapped to the three distinct claims (do NOT conflate):

| Claim | Metric(s) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Premise: divergence is real** | **Colocation Quotient** (+ permutation p) | Leslie & Kronenfeld 2011; Lee 2018; Hu 2018 |
| **(b) Model preserves divergence** (vs GAT collapse) | cross-type Spearman ρ on surfaces | in-house |
| **(c) Divergence not a grouping artifact** | threshold + scheme sensitivity (Tests 1–2) | MAUP / max-p-regions literature |
| **(d) Model actually predicts crashes** | **Site/Method Consistency Tests**; **crashes-captured@top-X%**; HSM network screening (EB/PSI); rank-validity ρ | Cheng & Washington 2005/2008; HSM (AASHTO) |
| **(e) Routing quality** | Spearman / Average Overlap / DCG | Sarraf 2020 (in wiki) |

Anchor the eval on **(1) crashes-captured@top-X% curve** (intuitive headline) and
**(2) Cheng-Washington site-consistency** (recognised-standard credibility).
