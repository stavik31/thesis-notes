# T-ITS — Carry-Over Deep-Read Record (Tier 1, Journal 1)

**What this is.** Full-text deep reads of the two IEEE T-ITS papers carried over from
Phase 2 into the Phase 3 routing direction: **Jiang et al. 2022** (the routing *home*) and
**Zhu et al. 2025** (the *niche premise*). Phase 2 had Jiang at one deep read and Zhu at
abstract-only; both are now read in full. Each entry has two layers: **Context grounding**
(where it places the thesis in the field — the Phase-2 job, continued) and **Technical

**Headline from these two reads:** the routing home is real and the gap is *named in print*,
but the vehicle-type premise is thinner than the carry-over assumed — Zhu is "vehicle-**group**,"
not "vehicle-**type**." See the gap test at the bottom.

## Jiang, Jafari, Kharbeche, Jalayer, Al-Khalifa 2022 — Safe Route Mapping of Roadways Using Multiple Sourced Data ★ HOME (the risk engine, not the router)

### Context grounding
This is the canonical "risk-assessment home" the supervisor named, in print: Safety
Performance Function (SPF) + Empirical Bayes (EB) + the AASHTO Highway Safety Manual (HSM).
It builds **dynamic risk heat maps** of roadways from historical crashes + driver-based
conflict data, and explicitly lists *"Safe Routes option in navigation using risk heat maps"*
as a target application. So the *deliverable shape* (a risk-scored map a driver could route
on) already exists — the raw idea is **not novel**.

The deep read sharpens two things the Phase-2 abstract could not:

1. **Jiang does NOT route.** It outputs a per-segment risk score in [0,100] and renders a
   heat map. There is no path-finding, no Dijkstra, no route ranking. The word "route" in the
   title refers to *mapping* roadway risk, not computing routes. Routing is deferred to future
   work: *"safe route recommendations for individual drivers can be realized using the risk heat
   maps generated by the SRM methodology."* → **Jiang is the risk ENGINE; the router is a
   separate piece** (that piece is Sarraf 2020, see [[ESWA-deepread]]). This is the single most
   useful structural correction from the deep read: our pipeline = Jiang-style engine → Sarraf-
   style router, and our novelty sits on *both* by conditioning each on vehicle type.

2. **Jiang names our exact gap, verbatim.** Quote (Section IV.B, on the driver model):
   *"risks are not dependent on the time of day, nor are they dependent on vehicle types or
   weather conditions."* The authors flag this as a limitation of their simulation, but it is
   precisely the dimension set the thesis claims — **vehicle type (primary), weather/time
   (secondary)**. A flagship-venue paper stating the absence in its own words is the cleanest
   possible "this is unoccupied" brick.

### Technical grounding (what's under the hood; what we borrow)
Three stacked models fused by fuzzy logic:

- **Crash-prediction model (the SPF):** Negative-Binomial (NB2) regression on historical
  crashes, derived properly from a Poisson→Gamma mixture to handle **over-dispersion**
  (variance > mean — the same rare-event problem we have). Exposure term = VMT (vehicle-miles
  travelled) with a nonlinear power γ. **Empirical Bayes** then forms a weighted average of the
  SPF estimate μ and the observed count N: `E(λ|N) = (1/(1+αμ))·μ + (1 − 1/(1+αμ))·N`, where α
  is the over-dispersion magnitude. This EB shrinkage is the HSM state-of-practice baseline our
  per-vehicle-type risk layer must either reuse or beat. Reported fit: Freeman-Tukey R² = 0.99
  on the case roadway (1,567 crashes, 2006–2017, 10 segments).
- **Driver-based model:** a small feed-forward NN (14 inputs → 10 hidden → sigmoid) predicting
  **conflict probability** (crash surrogate), trained on **simulated** VISSIM trajectories with
  SSAM conflict extraction (three conflict types: rear-end, crossing, lane-change; three driver
  types; three volume levels). AUC ≈ 0.81 test. Note: simulated, not real driver data — a
  stated limitation.
- **Integration:** fuzzy-logic reasoning (Gaussian membership functions, VL→VH, Min/Max
  operators, Center-of-Area defuzzification) collapses {crash count, conflict probability} into
  one risk score. This is *one* way to fuse a slow-changing historical signal with a fast-
  changing dynamic signal — relevant if we ever add a live layer, but heavier than we need for
  a historical-STATS19 build.

**Borrow:** NB/EB segment-risk modelling as the baseline engine; the severity-aware framing;
the heat-map-as-routing-substrate idea. **Replace / extend:** the aggregate (vehicle-blind)
risk score → per-vehicle-type risk; add the actual router (Jiang has none). **Skip:** the
fuzzy-logic integration and the VISSIM/SSAM simulated-conflict layer (we have real STATS19
counts, not simulated trajectories). Tag: `home` + `engine-precedent`.

## Zhu, Wang, Feng, Ma, Abdel-Aty 2025 — Vehicle-Group-Based Crash Risk Prediction and Interpretation on Highways ⚠ PREMISE, BUT WEAKER THAN BILLED

### Context grounding (with an important correction)
The carry-over filed this as the validator that "crash risk varies by vehicle grouping →
validates the vehicle-type niche." The full read forces a correction that matters for
positioning:

- **"Vehicle group" ≠ "vehicle type."** A VG here is a *dynamic cluster of interacting
  vehicles*, formed by an Impact-based Vehicle Grouping (IVG) method: vehicles are grouped when
  one's braking response to another's erratic manoeuvre exceeds a threshold. It is about who is
  *interacting with whom right now*, not about motorcycle-vs-HGV-vs-car. **Vehicle type is just
  one of eight node features** (Table II), not the subject of the paper.
- It is **microscopic, real-time, trajectory-based** (drone-captured MAGIC dataset, 25 Hz,
  urban expressway), using **iTTC** (inverse time-to-collision) surrogate safety measures — a
  *proactive/CAV* PTSM paradigm. It is **not** historical, segment-level, or STATS19-shaped, and
  it **does not route**.
- The authors' own stated limitation: they do *not* yet include "driver habits, weather, and
  road surface conditions" — i.e. the conditioning axes are future work for them too.

**Net effect on the thesis:** Zhu is a *weaker* niche-premise validator than the carry-over
implied — it does **not** establish "different vehicle classes face different *segment* risk."
So the vehicle-type-heterogeneity evidence base is still **THIN** and remains the priority of
the reading pass (need real AAP vehicle-type-risk papers: motorcycle/HGV segment risk). But
Zhu is a *stronger methodological cousin* than billed: it is a clean, top-venue example of
**graph-structured crash-risk modelling with explainability**, which is the same family our
engine lives in. The honest framing for the related-work chapter: cite Zhu (and the Abdel-Aty
lineage) as (a) the field anchor for real-time crash-risk and (b) precedent that *disaggregated,
per-entity* features (including vehicle type) beat aggregate features — while being explicit
that it predicts interaction risk, not per-type segment risk, and never routes.

### Technical grounding
- **Risk quantification:** iTTC between each leading/following pair; a VG's risk = aggregate of
  pairwise iTTC. Two targets: **Risk Indicator** (binary high-risk if max iTTC > 2/3 s⁻¹, i.e.
  TTC < 1.5 s) and **Risk Scale** (count of hazardous pairs). Threshold-based surrogate labels —
  not crash outcomes.
- **Models:** Logistic Regression on *aggregated* VG features vs. an **Edge-Conditioned
  Convolutional (ECC) GNN** (NNConv) on the *disaggregated* graph (nodes = vehicles, edges =
  pairwise risk). ECC chosen specifically because it ingests **edge attributes** (the inter-
  vehicle risk) — GAT/Graph-Transformer rejected for opacity; GraphSAGE/DGCNN rejected for
  ignoring edges. Both models AUC > 0.93 (risk level), ~0.85 (risk-scale trend).
- **Explainability:** **GNNExplainer** (learns a graph mask maximising mutual information with
  the prediction) + **feature perturbation** (zero a feature, watch the delta-output sign) to
  get directional attributions. Finding: vehicle *velocity* dominates (negative effect — lower
  speed = denser interactions = higher risk), then lane-change and acceleration; lead-vehicle
  behaviour drives risk *level*, central-vehicle behaviour drives risk *scale*.
- **Imbalance handling:** 8:1 class imbalance down-sampled to 4:1; 70:30 stratified split.

**Borrow:** the graph + post-hoc-XAI *evaluation discipline* (GNNExplainer/perturbation as a
template if our engine is a GNN; cf. Gao's GAT in [[AAP-deepread]]); the explicit "disaggregated
beats aggregated" argument as related-work ammunition for *why* conditioning helps. **Do not
lean on it** for the core vehicle-type-segment-risk premise. Tag: `niche-premise (weak)` +
`method-cousin` + `field-anchor (Abdel-Aty)`.

## Gap test (Phase 3): does anyone do vehicle-type-conditioned crash-risk *routing*?

- **Jiang 2022:** aggregate segment risk → heat map, **no routing, no vehicle type** (says so in
  print). NO.
- **Zhu 2025:** real-time interaction-cluster risk, vehicle type = 1 input feature, **no routing,
  not segment-level/historical**. NO.

Both "no"s, from different angles, are bricks for the niche. But the deep read also resets one
expectation: the *vehicle-type-heterogeneity* evidence (the premise that justifies routing by
type at all) is **not** supplied by Zhu and must be found elsewhere — that hunt is the live
priority. Structural takeaway carried into [[ESWA-deepread]]: **engine (Jiang) + router (Sarraf)
is the assembled stack; the thesis conditions both on vehicle type.**
